First, the basics

A neuron is just a switch.

  • It listens on a few wires.
  • Each wire has a number on it: how much that wire counts.
  • It adds up what it hears.
  • Big enough? It turns on. If not, it stays off.

That is the whole thing. Wire thousands of these together and you have a neural network. Nothing more exotic than that.

How it learns

Things that happen together get wired together.

  1. Show it a colour.
  2. Show it the right answer at the same moment.
  3. Some switches turn on. Some answer lights turn on.
  4. Every wire between two things that turned on together gets a bit stronger.
Neurons that fire together, wire together. Donald Hebb · 1949

Do that 4,000 times and it has learned the rule. Nothing ever measured how wrong it was. No score inside it, no correction, no maths pushing an error backwards.

All of them together

Only about 13 switch on at a time.

  • Left column: the colour goes in, smeared across 28 cells.
  • Middle: the 256. The bright dots are the ones firing.

The other 243 are not wrong. They just were not the loudest. They compete, and most of them lose.

The obvious question

Why such a small one?

  • This is about the smallest thing that still genuinely learns.
  • Small enough that you can see every single part of it.
  • You cannot point at one neuron inside a big model. Here you can.
  • Same ideas the big ones use. You can just watch them working.

A hatchling, not a toy. Simple on purpose, so nothing is hidden.

The part I did not expect

It knows when it does not know.

  • Some questions have two right answers.
  • It cannot pick one. So it votes for both at once.
  • Two humps instead of one. Confidence drops to half.

It was never taught what wrong means. It still tells you when it is unsure, and it is right about that almost every time.

Now break it

Kill most of it and it still works.

  • Kill 40% of the neurons. The score barely moves.
  • Kill 95%. Thirteen left out of 256. Still about half right.
  • Switch them back on and the score returns exactly.

Nothing in the code does this on purpose. Delete 40% of normal software and it does not run 99% as well.

Against a normal neural network

It loses on skill. It wins on damage.

  • Normal AI scores 97. This scores 85. Normal AI wins, clearly.
  • Then start killing neurons in both.
  • At 95% dead: this keeps 61%. Normal AI keeps 27%.

Neither one was built to survive damage. One just does.
