# Results

Generated by `node _dev/experiments.js`. Every number here comes from a seeded
run and regenerating this file reproduces it exactly.

- 4000 training examples per run, evaluation over 200 fresh colours
- the 64 probes in `probes.js` are excluded from every training stream
- the training stream is made reproducible by swapping the global `Math.random`
  for a seeded generator inside the harness only, because two of the six
  relations draw their randomness inside `apply()`
- total runtime 110.6 seconds

## What these say

**Confidence is a working uncertainty estimate.** Across 100 runs spanning every
relation that has a meaningful hue error, confidence correlates with actual hue error
at r = -0.97. High confidence goes with low error, and it goes with it hard.
Nothing in the network was told what the error was. There is no loss, no target
comparison, and no error signal anywhere in the code. The number falls out of how
sharply the output population agrees with itself. This is the strongest result here.

**Seed 7 was not lucky.** 85.3 ± 1.2 on `complement` across 20 seeds, range 84 to 88.
Ambiguous relations are consistently bad and unambiguous ones consistently good, which
is the point rather than a problem.

**chromaFraction 0.40 earned its place.** Of the four values tested it is the best:
0.2 scores 83.0, 0.4 scores 85.3, 0.6 scores 82.9, 0.8 scores 79.6.
The rationale asserted in the write-up survives being tested.

**Redundancy is real and it scales with size.** At 256 neurons, killing 40 per cent
costs 0.9 points and killing 95 per cent still leaves 49.9. At 96 neurons the
same cuts cost 7.4 and leave 34.8. Nothing implements fault tolerance. It is a
side effect of spreading the answer across many cells, and more cells means more of it.

### Three things that contradict the write-up

**The homeostatic cap does almost nothing at these settings.** `brain.js` says of it:
"Without this the brain collapses into shouting the same answer at everything."
Removing it costs 0.9 points. It is not idle either: 103 of 256 cells sit
pressed against it. The reason it can bind constantly and still not matter is that the
hue readout is a weighted circular mean, which divides by total weight, so it is
largely blind to how big any one cell's weights became. Removing the cap and the
competition together gives 79.0, so the predicted collapse does not happen even then.

**Sparse wiring does not help the score.** Dense wiring, connectivity 1.0, scores
86.5 against a baseline of 85.3, which is better, not worse.
It costs precision on hue, 4.2 degrees against 4.5. The claim that full connectivity
would make all 256 cells respond identically is false for this implementation: every
cell still gets its own independent signed random weights. They just become less
usefully different.

**Competition matters, but it does not prevent a collapse.** Turning k winners take all
off costs 5.2 points, which is the largest single ablation here and still not a
collapse. What stops runaway when the contest is off is the last line of `think()`,
which divides the whole layer by its own total activity so the layer always fires with
the same total energy. That normalisation is doing work usually credited to the contest.

### One correction to an earlier note in this repository

An earlier single-seed check concluded that lesion damage does not recover at all. With
ten seeds that is too strong. At 50 and 80 per cent there is nothing to recover, but at
90 per cent retraining the survivors regains 4.3 points and at 95 per cent 5.7 points,
which is 18.9 and 16.4 per cent of what was lost. Small, consistent, and not zero. The
ceiling is explained by `Wih` being fixed at birth: retraining can only readjust the
output weights, so it cannot rebuild tuning curves the dead cells took with them.

## 2. Ablations

Ten seeds each on `complement`, one thing removed at a time. Two of these are
reachable through config. The other two patch a copy of `brain.js` in memory,
dev only; the shipped file is not modified.

| condition | score | delta | hue error | delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 85.3 ± 1.1 | +0.0 | 4.2° | +0.0° |
| k winners take all off (fireFraction 1.0) | 80.1 ± 1.1 | -5.2 | 6.8° | +2.6° |
| sparse wiring off (connectivity 1.0) | 86.5 ± 0.8 | +1.2 | 4.5° | +0.3° |
| homeostatic cap removed | 84.4 ± 1.0 | -0.9 | 4.6° | +0.4° |
| mean input subtraction removed | 84.3 ± 1.2 | -1.0 | 5.1° | +0.9° |
| cap AND competition both removed | 79.0 ± 1.6 | -6.3 | 7.4° | +3.2° |

The cap is not idle: after 4000 examples, **103 of 256 cells** are pressed right against it. It binds constantly and
removing it still barely changes the score.

## 6. Lesion, then retrain the survivors

Ten seeds, `complement`. Lesion, measure, then train another 4000 examples
with the mask still on, and measure again.

The brief specified 50 per cent. That is included, but 50 per cent does no
measurable damage in the first place, so on its own it can only ever return a
null result. Deeper lesions are included so that there is something to recover.

| killed | before | lesioned | retrained | change | of the loss recovered |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50% | 85.3 | 83.6 | 83.0 | -0.6 | -35.3% |
| 80% | 85.3 | 76.3 | 76.4 | +0.1 | 1.1% |
| 90% | 85.3 | 62.5 | 66.8 | +4.3 | 18.9% |
| 95% | 85.3 | 50.6 | 56.3 | +5.7 | 16.4% |

## 7. Confidence calibration

Does the confidence number know when the network is wrong? Every run from
experiment 1 that has a meaningful hue error, which is all six relations except
`luminance`, where every answer is a grey.

| scope | n | correlation of confidence against hue error |
|---|---|---|
| all relations with a hue | 100 | -0.97 |
| complement alone | 20 | -0.41 |
| analogous alone | 20 | -0.45 |
| triadic alone | 20 | -0.42 |
| split-complement alone | 20 | -0.31 |
| warmer alone | 20 | -0.68 |

A strong negative correlation means high confidence goes with low error, which
is what a working uncertainty estimate looks like.
