# Your brief: **Split Complement**

## Your puzzle

Your brain has to learn to find the colour **just to one side of the
opposite**. Not the exact opposite — 150 degrees round, or 210 degrees round.

Both of those are correct answers, and the rule picks one of them at random
each time it shows your brain an example. They sit close together, either
side of the true opposite.

In `config.js`:

```js
relation: 'split-complement',
```

## Why yours is interesting

There is someone else in the room with the **triadic** puzzle. Their rule
also has two right answers — but theirs are far apart (120 degrees either
way) while yours are close together (only 30 degrees either side of the
opposite).

So the two of you are running the same experiment at two different
strengths. **Find that person early** and agree to compare results at the
end. Your two screens side by side are the most interesting thing in this
room.

Before you train: predict whether being *closer together* will make your
brain's job easier or harder than theirs.

## Things to watch

- **The confidence number**, and how it compares to both the triadic brain
  and to a brain with only one right answer (complement or analogous).
- **The brain map.** Your brain's answer will land somewhere specific
  relative to the two correct answers. Where exactly?
- Does your brain ever pick one of the two answers cleanly, or does it always
  do the same thing?

## If you finish early

Open `colors.js` and find your relation. The two answers are `150` and `210`
— that is 30 degrees either side of 180. **Widen the gap.** Try 120 and 240.
Then 90 and 270. Retrain each time and record the confidence.

You are measuring how ambiguity damages a brain, as a curve. Nobody told you
the shape of that curve — go and find it.
